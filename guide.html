IMPLEMENTED

Alliteration
Repetition of initial consonant sounds in successive or neighboring words.
"Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers"
Difficulty: EASY
Method: Phonetic algorithms (Metaphone/Soundex)
Challenges: Silent letters, homophones, word boundary detection

IMPLEMENTING NEXT

Anaphora
Repetition of the same word or phrase at the beginning of successive clauses or sentences.
"We shall fight on the beaches. We shall fight on the landing grounds. We shall fight in the fields."
Difficulty: EASY
Method: NLP parsing + pattern matching on sentence/clause starts
Challenges: Clause boundary detection, determining meaningful repetition length
Epistrophe
Repetition of the same word or phrase at the end of successive clauses or sentences.
"...government of the people, by the people, for the people"
Difficulty: EASY
Method: NLP parsing + pattern matching on sentence/clause ends
Challenges: Similar to anaphora but at clause endings
Epizeuxis
Immediate repetition of a word with no others between, for emphasis or emotion.
"Never, never, never give up"
Difficulty: EASY
Method: Simple token matching (adjacent word detection)
Challenges: Minimal - very straightforward pattern
Anadiplosis
Repetition where the last word of one clause becomes the first word of the next.
"Fear leads to anger. Anger leads to hate. Hate leads to suffering."
Difficulty: EASY
Method: Clause parsing + end-to-start matching
Challenges: Handling punctuation, determining clause boundaries
Tricolon
Three parallel elements of approximately equal length appearing in succession.
"Government of the people, by the people, for the people"
Difficulty: MEDIUM
Method: Isocolon detection + counting (must be exactly 3)
Challenges: Same as isocolon, plus enforcing exactly three elements
Rhetorical Question
A question asked for effect or emphasis rather than to obtain information.
"Is the pope Catholic?"
Difficulty: MEDIUM
Method: Question detection + heuristics (negative tags, obvious answers)
Challenges: Context-dependent, requires understanding of implied answers

TIER 1: Other High-Impact Devices

Assonance
Repetition of vowel sounds in stressed syllables of nearby words.
"The rain in Spain stays mainly in the plain"
Difficulty: MEDIUM
Method: Phonetic analysis + stress detection
Challenges: Determining stress patterns, vowel pronunciation variability
Consonance
Repetition of consonant sounds with different vowels in nearby words.
"The lumpy, bumpy road"
Difficulty: MEDIUM
Method: Phonetic pattern matching
Challenges: Distinguishing from alliteration, position independence
Isocolon
A series of parallel clauses or phrases of approximately equal length.
"Veni, vidi, vici" (I came, I saw, I conquered)
Difficulty: MEDIUM
Method: NLP parsing + length comparison + structure matching
Challenges: Defining "equal length" (words vs syllables), detecting parallel structure
Antithesis
Juxtaposition of contrasting ideas in parallel grammatical structures.
"One small step for man, one giant leap for mankind"
Difficulty: HARD
Method: Parallel structure detection + semantic opposition analysis
Challenges: Identifying semantic opposites (requires word embeddings or antonym database)
Chiasmus
Reversed grammatical structure in successive phrases or clauses (ABBA pattern).
"Ask not what your country can do for you—ask what you can do for your country"
Difficulty: HARD
Method: Deep syntactic parsing + structure reversal detection
Challenges: Requires accurate POS tagging, handling syntactic variations

TIER 2: Medium Complexity (Add Later)

Polyptoton
Repetition of words derived from the same root but with different forms.
"The strong strengthens the strength"
Difficulty: MEDIUM
Method: Lemmatization + root word matching
Challenges: Accurate lemmatization, distinguishing from simple word repetition
Antimetabole
Repetition of words in reverse order in successive clauses.
"Fair is foul, and foul is fair"
Difficulty: MEDIUM
Method: Word sequence matching + reversal detection
Challenges: Distinguishing from chiasmus (word vs structure reversal)
Symploce
Combination of anaphora and epistrophe—repetition at both beginning and end.
"When there is talk of hatred, let us stand up and talk against it. When there is talk of violence, let us stand up and talk against it."
Difficulty: MEDIUM
Method: Combined anaphora + epistrophe detection
Challenges: Must detect both patterns simultaneously
Asyndeton
Omission of conjunctions between series elements, creating a rushed or emphatic effect.
"I came, I saw, I conquered"
Difficulty: MEDIUM
Method: List detection + conjunction absence checking
Challenges: Distinguishing intentional omission from normal comma lists
Polysyndeton
Deliberate use of many conjunctions for effect (opposite of asyndeton).
"...and the wind and the rain and the cold"
Difficulty: EASY
Method: Conjunction counting in series
Challenges: Determining threshold for "excessive" conjunctions

TIER 3: Semantic/Complex (Probably Too Hard)

Paradox
A seemingly contradictory statement that contains a measure of truth.
"Less is more"
Difficulty: VERY HARD
Method: Semantic analysis + logical contradiction detection
Challenges: Requires deep semantic understanding and world knowledge
Oxymoron
Adjacent words with seemingly contradictory meanings.
"Deafening silence", "jumbo shrimp"
Difficulty: HARD
Method: Semantic opposition detection on adjacent words
Challenges: Requires semantic database, distinguishing from metaphorical language
Irony
Using language to convey meaning opposite to the literal interpretation.
"What a beautiful day!" (said during a thunderstorm)
Difficulty: VERY HARD
Method: Context analysis + sentiment/reality mismatch detection
Challenges: Requires external context, tone detection, cultural knowledge
Antanaclasis
Repetition of a word in two different senses (pun on meanings).
"If we don't hang together, we'll hang separately"
Difficulty: VERY HARD
Method: Word sense disambiguation + multiple meaning detection
Challenges: Requires comprehensive dictionary with multiple definitions, context analysis

TIER 4: Impractical (Reference Only)

Homoioptoton
Repetition of similar case endings in adjacent words (Greek/Latin).
"Tempora mutantur, nos et mutamur in illis"
Difficulty: VERY HARD
Method: Morphological analysis of inflected languages
Challenges: English lacks case system; requires classical language support
Homoioteleuton
Similarity of word endings in adjacent or parallel words.
"Surely it is destruction, not construction, at which you aim"
Difficulty: HARD
Method: Suffix pattern matching
Challenges: Very common in English (-tion, -ly), hard to distinguish from coincidence
Paroemion
Extreme alliteration where every word begins with the same consonant.
"Pourquoi pas produire promptement ce procès?" (French example)
Difficulty: EASY
Method: Same as alliteration but stricter (100% match rate)
Challenges: Extremely rare in natural speech, not worth detecting
Mesarchia
Repetition of words at the beginning and middle of successive sentences.
"Time heals all wounds. Time is what we have. Time will tell."
Difficulty: MEDIUM
Method: Position-based repetition detection
Challenges: Defining "middle" is vague, rarely used consciously
Mesodiplosis
Repetition of the same word(s) in the middle of successive sentences.
"The art of peace begins in the heart. Victory in war begins in the heart."
Difficulty: MEDIUM
Method: Middle-position word matching
Challenges: Too subtle, low rhetorical impact
Metathesis
Transposition of letters within a word.
"Aks" instead of "ask"
Difficulty: EASY
Method: Letter-order comparison against dictionary
Challenges: Usually considered an error, not a rhetorical device
Tmesis
Inserting a word between parts of a compound word.
"A whole nother story" (instead of "another")
Difficulty: MEDIUM
Method: Compound word database + insertion detection
Challenges: Very rare, requires comprehensive compound word list
Hysteron Proteron
Reversing the natural chronological order of events.
"I die, I faint, I fail" (death comes after fainting/failing)
Difficulty: VERY HARD
Method: Temporal reasoning + world knowledge
Challenges: Requires understanding of real-world event sequences
Commoratio
Dwelling repeatedly on one's strongest argument.
(Conceptual rather than structural—entire passages)
Difficulty: VERY HARD
Method: Argument structure analysis + topic modeling
Challenges: Requires discourse-level understanding, not sentence-level
Exergasia
Repeating the same thought using multiple rhetorical figures.
(Meta-device about using other devices)
Difficulty: VERY HARD
Method: Requires detecting all other devices first + semantic similarity
Challenges: Meta-level analysis, circular dependency
